AI-Enabled Video Workflows

AI handles the slow first passes of production, so more time goes into story, pacing, and polish. The editorial decisions stay with me.

Timeline assembly

Interviews and lectures are transcribed automatically. I edit the transcript to pull selects, cut filler and dead air, and assemble a first rough cut, so hours of footage become a working timeline in minutes.

AI-generated footage

When filming isn't practical, I generate short b-roll from text prompts, such as establishing shots, abstract backgrounds, and concept visuals. Each clip is color-matched and trimmed to sit naturally alongside the filmed footage.

AI-generated imagery

I create custom stills for thumbnails, title cards, slide backgrounds, and lower thirds, and use generative fill to extend or clean up photos so they fit any aspect ratio.

Accessibility

Accessible media reaches more people and works better for everyone. I plan for it from the first draft instead of fixing it at the end.

Captions and transcripts

Every video gets accurate closed captions, delivered as a caption file through the player so viewers can turn them on or off. Lecture videos also come with a transcript.

Readable on-screen text

Titles and lower thirds meet WCAG AA contrast, stay inside the title-safe area, and hold on screen long enough to read twice.

Accessible course pages

Canvas pages use real headings, descriptive link text, alt text, and tables with header rows, so they work with screen readers and keyboards.

Comfortable motion and sound

No rapid flashing, motion that serves the message, clear dialogue mixes, and described visuals whenever an image carries key information.

Contrast check

Text and background pairs from the three campaigns, scored against WCAG 2.2. Switch the view to see how each palette holds up for color-blind viewers.
